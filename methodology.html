How this trail is built

Methodology

This site reports declared values: what a vendor's own page said on a given date. It does not verify billing, average anything, or fill missing values. Its job is to make changes — especially quiet reversals — as legible as launch announcements.

The evidence model

  • Captures. Each monitored page is fetched at build time, saved raw, and pinned by a SHA-256 receipt. Capture files are published alongside this site so any quote can be re-checked against its source. Pages that change per request carry no stable hash promise — the receipt identifies the exact fetch, not a forever-identity.
  • Monitoring notes. Dated observations recorded while tracking the pages (e.g., "rate basis as of 2026-08-31"). They carry dates and are labeled as notes — never presented as vendor quotes.
  • Popover notes. Some vendor wording appears only in popovers — a footnote on a price cell, or a model's status note. It is on the page a reader sees, so it counts as captured page text, and each quote's label says where it appears.
  • Layout changes stop the build. Price tables are read by column label, never by position. If a vendor re-lays out a table and an expected label disappears, the build fails rather than publishing a price from the wrong column.
  • Provisional items. Where a vendor's pages cannot be captured, third-party reports are listed as provisional and labeled — never as vendor-verified.

Rules this trail follows

  • A failed fetch is a flagged finding, never silence. If a source cannot be fetched, the transport errors are recorded and displayed; the source becomes a documented gap.
  • Hard per-source deadlines. Every fetch runs under a wall-clock deadline with a second transport fallback. A hanging server becomes a recorded error, never a stall — and one transport's failure is never treated as "the page moved" until both fail.
  • Quotes are verified at build time. Every quote shown for an event is checked against the captured page text; the build fails closed on a quote it cannot locate.
  • Current and historical bases are separate. A current page price never gets used to date a past period. Where a transition date is only known from a historical notice, the note says so.
  • Absent evidence is never a clean bill. "No reversal found" is only claimable over the capture interval; vendors without a capture are documented gaps.
  • Quiet moves stay quiet. Where a change has no published announcement date, the timeline says so and shows the first date it became visible in captures.

What this is not

  • Not investment or procurement advice; no vendor recommendations.
  • Not invoice reconciliation — declared prices are not billed prices.
  • Not price prediction, and not rate-limit or uptime monitoring beyond what a snapshot evidences.
  • No accounts, no tracking, no backend: a static site and dated files.

Update cadence

The dataset is rebuilt by fetching the vendor pages and re-pinning captures; each build stamps its own dates. Reversals between builds are only visible to the extent a capture or monitoring note recorded them — which is exactly the asymmetry this watch exists to shrink.
